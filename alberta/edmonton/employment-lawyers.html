What employment lawyers in Edmonton help with

Employment lawyers advise employees and employers on termination and severance, employment contracts, workplace rights and disputes.

  • Being terminated, laid off or constructively dismissed
  • Negotiating a severance package
  • Reviewing an employment contract or non-compete
  • Harassment, discrimination or human rights issues at work
  • Unpaid wages, overtime or vacation pay
  • Advising employers on policies and terminations

Employment law in Alberta: what to know

Alberta's Limitations Act sets a basic two-year limitation period for most claims, with exceptions. Speak to a lawyer early so that a deadline does not run out.

Employment rights in Alberta come from the Employment Standards Code and from your contract and the common law, so your entitlement may be more than the statutory minimum.

How to choose a employment lawyer in Edmonton

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Law Society of Alberta.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is employment, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • Is my severance offer reasonable, and can it be improved?
  • How long do I have to respond to the offer?
  • Do you act for employees, employers or both?
  • What would a claim involve, and what might it cost?
  • Are you paid by the hour, a flat fee or a percentage?
  • Should I sign anything before we speak again?

What to bring

  • Your employment contract and any amendments
  • Your termination letter or severance offer
  • Recent pay stubs and records of benefits
  • Performance reviews and relevant emails
  • A timeline of events

How the process usually works

  1. You share your documents and the lawyer reviews them.
  2. They advise on your entitlements and the strength of your position.
  3. A response or counter-offer is prepared.
  4. The lawyer negotiates with your employer or their lawyer.
  5. If needed, a claim is filed.

Costs and fees

Some employment lawyers offer a free or low-cost first review of a severance offer, and some work for a percentage of any improvement. Ask before you commit. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
