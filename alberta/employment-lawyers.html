About employment law in Alberta

Employment lawyers advise employees and employers on termination and severance, employment contracts, workplace rights and disputes.

Alberta's Limitations Act sets a basic two-year limitation period for most claims, with exceptions. Speak to a lawyer early so that a deadline does not run out.

Employment rights in Alberta come from the Employment Standards Code and from your contract and the common law, so your entitlement may be more than the statutory minimum.

Employment lawyers in Alberta: a short guide

What employment lawyers help with

  • Being fired or laid off, and severance
  • Constructive dismissal
  • Employment contracts and non-compete clauses
  • Workplace harassment and discrimination
  • Unpaid wages, overtime and vacation pay
  • Advice for employers on policies and terminations

How to choose a employment lawyer

  1. Decide whether you are an employee or an employer, then pick a firm that acts for that side.
  2. Ask how soon the firm can review a termination letter or an offer, since time can matter.
  3. Check the rating and recent reviews for how clearly the firm explains options.
  4. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society.

Questions to ask at your first call

  • What is a fair package in my situation, and how is it worked out?
  • Should I sign what I have been given?
  • What time limits apply to a claim?
  • What would the process and cost look like if this does not settle?
  • What should I avoid doing or saying in the meantime?

What to bring

  • Your employment contract and any amendments
  • The termination letter or offer, if you have one
  • Pay stubs and records of bonuses and benefits
  • Performance reviews and relevant emails
  • A timeline of events at work

How the process usually works

  1. You share your documents and the lawyer reviews them.
  2. They advise on your entitlements and the strength of your position.
  3. A response or counter-offer is prepared.
  4. The lawyer negotiates with your employer or their lawyer.
  5. If needed, a claim is filed.

What does a employment lawyer cost in Alberta?

Some employment lawyers offer a free or low-cost first review of a severance offer, and some work for a percentage of any improvement. Ask before you commit. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
