About litigation law in Alberta

Litigation lawyers act in civil disputes: contract and commercial claims, shareholder and partnership disputes, debt recovery and other cases that may end up in court or settled before trial.

Alberta's Limitations Act sets a basic two-year limitation period for most claims, with exceptions. Speak to a lawyer early so that a deadline does not run out.

Civil disputes in Alberta are heard in the Provincial Court and the Court of King's Bench of Alberta.

Litigation lawyers in Alberta: a short guide

What litigation lawyers help with

  • Contract and business disputes
  • Claims for unpaid money or damage to property
  • Construction and real property disputes
  • Shareholder and partnership disputes
  • Defending a lawsuit that has been filed against you
  • Settlement negotiations, mediation and arbitration

How to choose a litigation lawyer

  1. Look for a firm that has handled disputes like yours, and ask about the courts or tribunals it appears in.
  2. Ask how the firm weighs the cost of a case against what is at stake.
  3. Check the firm's rating and recent reviews for communication during a long matter.
  4. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society.

Questions to ask at your first call

  • What are the realistic options, including settling without a trial?
  • How is the work billed, and how often will I get an update?
  • What are the time limits that apply to my claim?
  • What could the total cost look like at each stage?
  • Who will be working on my file?

What to bring

  • Contracts, invoices, emails and messages about the dispute
  • A timeline of what happened and when
  • Any letters or legal documents you have received
  • The names of everyone involved
  • Photo identification

How the process usually works

  1. The lawyer reviews your documents and explains your options.
  2. A demand letter or statement of claim (or defence) is prepared.
  3. Both sides exchange documents and information.
  4. Mediation or settlement talks often take place.
  5. If the case does not settle, it proceeds to trial and a decision.

Common questions

How do I choose a litigation lawyer in Alberta?

Look for a firm that has handled disputes like yours, and ask about the courts or tribunals it appears in. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society.

What does a litigation lawyer cost in Alberta?

Litigation is most often billed hourly. Ask for a budget by stage, and what happens to fees if the case settles early. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
