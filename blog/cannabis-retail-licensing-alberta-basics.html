Cannabis in Alberta: How Retail Licensing and Federal Rules Fit Together

Torys says its Calgary clients include cannabis growers, alongside oil and gas and power companies and renewable energy generators. This guide is our own general overview.

Cannabis is regulated at two levels in Canada. The federal government licenses production, while each province decides how the product is sold. Alberta chose a private retail model, with a provincial regulator overseeing it.

Federal production licences

Growing, processing and selling cannabis at the wholesale level requires licences from Health Canada under the federal Cannabis Act. The licensing process covers security, facilities, record-keeping and the people involved in the business.

Licence holders have ongoing obligations on quality, labelling, packaging and reporting.

Alberta retail and distribution

In Alberta the Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Commission (AGLC) licenses private retailers and oversees the provincial distribution system. Retail licences come with conditions on location, store design, staff and record-keeping.

Municipal bylaws, including zoning and separation distances, may also affect where a shop can operate.

Deals and compliance

Investors and buyers in the sector look closely at licence status, whether changes of ownership or control need regulator approval, and compliance history. Financing can be harder than in other industries, so lender requirements should be checked early.

Before buying or opening a cannabis business

  • Verify the federal and provincial licence status and any conditions.
  • Check whether a change of ownership or control needs regulator approval.
  • Confirm zoning and separation distances with the municipality.
  • Ask lenders early what they will require.

Frequently asked questions

Who licenses cannabis growers?

Health Canada, under the federal Cannabis Act.

Who licenses cannabis shops in Alberta?

The Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Commission.

Are municipal rules relevant?

Yes. Zoning and other municipal bylaws can affect where a shop can operate.

Does a change of ownership need approval?

Often yes, so check the licence conditions and regulator requirements before closing.

Why do buyers do compliance due diligence?

Because licence history and compliance records can affect whether a licence is safe to acquire.
