Mediation or Arbitration? Resolving a Business Dispute in Alberta Without a Trial

Bishop & McKenzie publishes on litigation, mediation and arbitration among its topics. This guide is our own general overview.

Going to trial is slow and costly, so many Alberta disputes are resolved another way. Mediation and arbitration are the two main options, and choosing between them depends on how much control you want to keep.

Mediation

In mediation a neutral person helps the parties negotiate. The mediator does not decide the outcome, and any settlement is voluntary. It is confidential, usually much quicker than court and preserves relationships better than a fight over liability.

It works best when both sides are willing to compromise and have enough information to value the claim.

Arbitration

In arbitration a neutral arbitrator or panel hears the case and issues a binding decision, a process governed in Alberta by the Arbitration Act and by any arbitration clause in the contract. It is private and can be tailored, but arbitrators charge fees and appeal rights are limited.

Check your contract, since many commercial agreements already require arbitration.

Court-connected options

Even in a court case, judicial dispute resolution lets a judge help the parties explore settlement. It is not the trial and the judge will not decide the case if it does not settle.

Whichever route you use, gather your documents early and identify the real issues, since that saves time and cost.

Choosing a route

  • Read your contract for an arbitration or mediation clause first.
  • Decide whether you want a decision or a negotiated result.
  • Ask for a cost estimate for each route, including neutral fees.
  • Prepare a short chronology and document set before you start.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between mediation and arbitration?

A mediator helps you negotiate but cannot decide, while an arbitrator decides and the decision is binding.

Is mediation confidential?

Generally yes, though the details depend on the agreement to mediate.

Can I appeal an arbitration decision?

Only in limited circumstances.

Does my contract already require arbitration?

Possibly. Check for an arbitration or dispute resolution clause.

What is judicial dispute resolution?

A settlement process led by a judge within the court system, separate from the trial.
