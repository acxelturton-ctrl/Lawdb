ICBC and Personal Injury Claims in Vancouver: What No-Fault Insurance Actually Changed

British Columbia is a public auto insurance province - every driver is insured through ICBC, not a private insurer of their choice - and in 2021 ICBC shifted most drivers onto what it calls the Enhanced Care model. That change significantly limited the ability to sue an at-fault driver directly for pain and suffering after most collisions, which is a real departure from how injury claims work in provinces with private insurance and tort-based systems.

What Enhanced Care actually covers

Under Enhanced Care, an injured person generally receives medical, wage-loss, and rehabilitation benefits directly from ICBC regardless of who caused the crash, without needing to prove fault or sue anyone. What it removes, for most collisions, is the right to sue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering - the non-economic damages that used to be the centrepiece of a BC injury claim and the reason so many were litigated rather than settled quietly.

Where the right to sue still exists

The model carves out exceptions for certain categories - crashes involving a driver who was impaired, uninsured, or engaged in specific serious violations, among other circumstances defined in the legislation. Whether a given crash falls into one of those categories isn't always obvious from the outset, which is exactly the kind of threshold question a personal injury lawyer needs to assess early, since it determines whether the claim proceeds as a benefits claim against ICBC or as a lawsuit against the other driver.

Why this catches people off guard

A lot of what people know about injury claims comes from advertising and media that reflects the tort-based system used in provinces like Ontario, where suing after a crash is the norm. Someone injured in a Vancouver collision who assumes the same process applies here is often surprised to learn the claim runs through ICBC's benefits system by default - which is exactly why an early conversation with a BC-specific injury lawyer matters more here than in most provinces.
