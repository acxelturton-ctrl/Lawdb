Court Decisions Explained

Recent Canadian court decisions, explained in plain language. Every summary here covers a real, published ruling, identified by its case citation so you can look up the full decision yourself.

Criminal DefenceOctober 9, 2026

Supreme Court Sets a Sentencing Framework When Both Offender and Victim Are Indigenous

In a 5-4 decision, the Court set a three-step approach for sentencing an Indigenous offender who harmed an Indigenous partner, requiring judges to apply Gladue principles and victim-protection principles together.

R. v. Cope, Supreme Court of Canada, October 9, 2026 (appeal from the Nova Scotia Court of Appeal)
Civil LitigationSeptember 2026

Ransomware Attack Falls Below the Deductible: Ontario Court of Appeal Sides With Insurer

The Ontario Court of Appeal held that a higher US$3 million ransomware retention in a cyber policy endorsement applied to a company's claim, leaving a roughly US$2 million loss entirely uninsured.

Panasonic Canada Inc. v. XL Specialty Insurance Company, 2026 ONCA 633
Employment LawSeptember 2026

Replaced Building Manager Can Claim Notice From the New Service Provider, Ontario Court of Appeal Confirms

The Ontario Court of Appeal dismissed an appeal and cross-appeal and held that a new building services provider is responsible for a former employee's common law notice entitlements, not only statutory termination pay.

Kondaj v. Crossbridge Condominium Services Ltd., 2026 ONCA 636 (appeal from 2025 ONSC 3905)
Civil LitigationJuly 31, 2026

Parliament Cannot Shield Government Decisions From Court Review, Supreme Court Rules

In a unanimous ruling with wide implications for anyone dealing with a government tribunal, the court struck down a statutory clause that tried to block judicial review, holding that the Constitution guarantees courts the final say on whether government action is lawful.

Democracy Watch v. Canada (Attorney General), 2026 SCC 28
Civil LitigationJuly 31, 2026

Destroying Evidence Once a Lawsuit Is Coming: Supreme Court Sets Out How Courts Should Respond

In a dispute over a software data licence, the Court set out a four-part test for spoliation, confirmed that courts must draw an adverse inference once it is proven, and sent a US$5.7 million damages award back for reassessment.

SS&C Technologies Canada Corp. v. Bank of New York Mellon Corporation, 2026 SCC 29
Criminal DefenceJuly 10, 2026

Supreme Court Upholds Mandatory Minimum for Paying for Sexual Services From a Minor

A majority of the Court held that the six-month mandatory minimum for obtaining sexual services from a person under 18 does not violate the Charter ban on cruel and unusual punishment, and explained how courts should build hypothetical cases.

Quebec (Attorney General) v. Denis, 2026 SCC 25
Criminal DefenceJune 5, 2026

Supreme Court Warns Trial Judges Against Turning Sexual Assault Trials Into Credibility Contests

The Court unanimously held that a credibility test used by appeal courts should not be used by trial judges to structure their reasoning, because it can undermine the presumption of innocence.

R. v. Berg, 2026 SCC 21
Real EstateMay 28, 2026

Top Court Declines to Revisit Ruling That Aboriginal Title Cannot Cover Private Land

The Supreme Court refused to hear a First Nation's appeal of a New Brunswick ruling that Aboriginal title cannot be declared over privately owned land - a decision the federal government says will shape arguments in the ongoing Cowichan litigation in British Columbia.

Leave to appeal denied, May 28, 2026 (New Brunswick Court of Appeal decision stands)
Family LawMay 15, 2026

Supreme Court Creates a New Civil Claim for Intimate Partner Violence

Canada's highest court has recognised a standalone civil claim for intimate partner violence, allowing survivors to sue for damages over a pattern of coercive and controlling behaviour rather than only over individual physical incidents.

Ahluwalia v. Ahluwalia, 2026 SCC 16
Civil LitigationMay 8, 2026

Can You Sue Over Something You Could Have Raised Earlier? Supreme Court Clarifies Cause of Action Estoppel

The Court restated the four-part test for cause of action estoppel (a branch of res judicata) and confirmed that a party who could have raised a claim earlier with reasonable diligence can be barred from raising it later.

Patrick Street Holdings Ltd. v. 11368 NL Inc., 2026 SCC 15
Real EstateJanuary 30, 2026

Insurance Payout Does Not Have to Cover Rebuilding Upgrades Required by Regulators

Homeowners whose flooded house was a total loss could not claim the extra cost of meeting a conservation authority's rebuilding requirements, because their policy specifically excluded costs of complying with regulations.

Emond v. Trillium Mutual Insurance Co., 2026 SCC 3
Criminal DefenceJanuary 23, 2026

Supreme Court Tightens How a Jury Can Hear About an Accused's Criminal Record

The court clarified how trial judges should weigh an accused person's past convictions before letting a jury hear about them, holding that youth records and prior violent offences should have been kept out - though the conviction still stood.

R. v. Hussein, 2026 SCC 2
