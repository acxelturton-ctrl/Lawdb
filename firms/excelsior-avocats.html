Married or not

01Do unmarried couples have the same rights as married couples in Quebec?

It depends on whether you are married, in a civil union, or living together without being married (a de facto couple).

  • Married or in a civil union: the family patrimony rules (Civil Code of Québec, arts. 391–400) and the right to claim support apply.
  • De facto couple: there is no automatic division of property and no automatic right to spousal support, however long you lived together. Assets generally stay with whoever holds title, unless you have a cohabitation agreement.
02What is the new parental union regime?

Since June 30, 2025, de facto spouses who become parents of the same child (born or adopted after that date) are automatically in a parental union. On separation, the net value of the family residence, household furnishings and family vehicles acquired after the child’s birth or adoption is divided 50/50. Couples can opt out or change the terms by notarial deed, and couples with earlier children can opt in by agreement.

The regime does not create a spousal support obligation.

Money & contact

03Does child support depend on whether we were married?

Yes. Child support follows the same rules for married and unmarried parents, and is set using the Québec child support calculation model.

04What can I claim if we were not married?

If you did not marry and are not covered by the parental union regime, a claim for unjust enrichment (Civil Code, art. 1493) may be possible when one partner contributed to the other’s assets. These claims generally must be filed within 3 years, so it is worth speaking to a lawyer promptly after separating.

Before you call about family law / divorce

General guidance for this kind of matter, not advice about your situation and not specific to this firm.

Questions worth asking

  • Do you push toward settlement or mediation first, or straight to court?
  • What is your realistic estimate of total cost for a matter like mine?
  • How are decisions about the children handled if we cannot agree?
  • How quickly do you respond, and who do I contact when you are in court?

How this work is usually billed

Family work is usually hourly against a retainer you top up as it is drawn down. Uncontested separations are sometimes offered as a flat fee. The cost gap between a negotiated agreement and a contested trial is very large, so ask about both.

What to have ready

Dates of marriage and separation, a rough list of assets and debts, recent pay information for both parties, and any agreement already signed.
