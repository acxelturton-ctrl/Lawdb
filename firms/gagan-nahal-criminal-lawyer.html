Driving prohibitions

01I was given a roadside driving prohibition in BC. How long does it last?

The immediate roadside prohibition (IRP) is an administrative driving ban issued at the roadside. Its length depends on the screening-device result:

  • “Fail” reading (over .08): 90 days.
  • “Warn” reading (over .05): 3 days for a first prohibition, 7 days for a second, 30 days for any later one.

A criminal impaired driving charge is a separate process with its own court dates.

02Can I challenge a roadside prohibition?

You can ask Road Safety BC for a review within 7 days of receiving the notice. You apply on a form available at any ICBC driver licensing office and pay a fee. A decision is usually made within 21 days of receiving the notice, and you remain prohibited from driving while the review is pending.

Court delay & contact

03Is there a limit on how long my criminal case can take?

Under the Supreme Court of Canada’s decision in R. v. Jordan (2016), delay beyond a presumptive ceiling is treated as unreasonable: 18 months for cases in provincial court and 30 months for cases in superior court (or in provincial court after a preliminary inquiry). If the ceiling is exceeded, the Crown must justify the delay; if it cannot, the charges can be stayed. Delay caused by the defence does not count.

Before you call about criminal charges

General guidance for this kind of matter, not advice about your situation and not specific to this firm.

Questions worth asking

  • Have you handled this specific charge before, and how did those matters end?
  • What are the realistic outcomes here, including the ones I will not like?
  • Who will actually appear in court, you or someone else at the firm?
  • What happens to my case if it goes past the stage your quote covers?

How this work is usually billed

Criminal defence is usually billed as a block fee tied to a stage of the case, bail hearing, resolution discussions, trial, rather than one price for everything. Get in writing which stage your fee covers.

What to have ready

Your release documents or promise to appear, the disclosure package if the Crown has provided it, and the date of your next court appearance.
