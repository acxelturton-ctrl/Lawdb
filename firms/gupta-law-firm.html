Deadlines & claims

01I was hurt in a car accident in Ontario. What deadlines should I know about?

Three clocks start almost immediately.

  • 7 days: tell your own auto insurer you intend to claim accident benefits.
  • 30 days: return the completed accident benefits application, counted from when you receive the forms from your insurer.
  • 2 years: the usual limit to sue the at-fault driver, counted from the accident or from when you discovered the injury (Ontario’s Limitations Act, 2002).

Missing the early deadlines can reduce or end your benefits, so it is worth speaking to a lawyer in the first week rather than the first year.

02What is the difference between accident benefits and a personal injury lawsuit?

Accident benefits are no-fault: they come from your own insurer whoever caused the crash, and cover things like medical and rehabilitation costs and income replacement.

A personal injury lawsuit is a separate claim against the at-fault party, usually paid by their insurer, for losses such as pain and suffering and income loss beyond what benefits cover. Many people have both running at once, each with its own deadlines.

03I fell on a city sidewalk or road. Is there an extra deadline?

Yes. For injuries on municipal property in Ontario, the Municipal Act requires written notice to the municipality, and that deadline can be as short as 10 days. It is separate from, and much shorter than, the 2-year limit to sue.

While you wait to speak to a lawyer: photograph the exact spot and conditions, note the date and time, and keep the shoes and clothing you were wearing.

Courts & costs

04Where would my claim be heard if I live in Peel Region?

Civil cases from Peel are typically heard at the Brampton courthouse. Claims up to $50,000 (the limit rose from $35,000 on October 1, 2025) go to Small Claims Court at 7765 Hurontario Street. Larger claims, which includes most serious injury cases, go to the Superior Court of Justice at 7755 Hurontario Street.

Gupta Law Firm’s office, at 2250 Bovaird Dr E #314, is in Brampton.

05How do personal injury lawyers usually charge in Ontario, and what should I ask Gupta Law Firm?

Many personal injury lawyers in Ontario work on a contingency basis, meaning their fee comes out of the recovery if the case succeeds, and the agreement should be in writing. Terms vary firm to firm.

Before you sign anything, ask: what percentage is charged, how out-of-pocket costs (disbursements) are handled, and what, if anything, you owe if there is no recovery. LawDB does not list Gupta Law Firm’s fees, so ask on your first call.

Before you call about personal injury / accident

General guidance for this kind of matter, not advice about your situation and not specific to this firm.

Questions worth asking

  • Do you work on contingency, and what percentage, calculated before or after disbursements?
  • Who covers costs like medical reports if the claim does not succeed?
  • How many matters like mine have you resolved, and how long did they take?
  • Will you handle my file personally?

How this work is usually billed

Almost always contingency: no fee unless you recover. The number that matters is not just the percentage but whether it is calculated before or after disbursements, and who is liable for those if you lose.

What to have ready

Accident and police reports, all medical records and treatment notes, correspondence from any insurer, and a record of income you have lost.
