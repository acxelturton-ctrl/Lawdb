Soul Family Law is a Winnipeg practice handling separation, divorce, custody, support and property division through the collaborative method.

Ms. Soul was admitted to the Manitoba bar in 2009 and has practised collaborative law since training in 2011. In the collaborative process, spouses agree not to file court materials and negotiate with their own collaboratively trained lawyers, sometimes alongside communication or parenting coaches, a financial professional or a child-focused adviser. If either spouse later chooses court, written notice is required and both must retain new lawyers. Meetings are held remotely over Zoom or by voice link.

Before you call about a separation or divorce

General guidance for this kind of matter, not advice about your situation and not specific to this firm.

Questions worth asking

  • Do you handle negotiation and mediation as well as court, and which do you usually recommend for a situation like mine?
  • What will you need from me in the first month, and what is the likely timeline?
  • How do you charge, and is a retainer required up front?
  • What can I do now to protect my position on parenting and finances?

How this work is usually billed

Family lawyers usually bill hourly against a retainer, though some offer limited-scope or flat-fee services for simple matters such as an uncontested divorce. Ask what is included and how often you will receive an account. Legal Aid Manitoba may help if you qualify financially.

What to have ready

Your marriage or cohabitation dates, the date you separated, recent income information for both spouses, a list of assets and debts, and any existing agreements or court orders. Family matters in Manitoba are heard in the Court of King’s Bench (Family Division), and many disputes settle before a hearing.

You can confirm that any lawyer is licensed through the Law Society of Manitoba.
