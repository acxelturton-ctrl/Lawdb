Multiple Wills: Why Some Ontario Estates Use More Than One

Ontario charges estate administration tax on the value of assets passing through probate. Using more than one will can keep certain assets out of that calculation entirely.

What the second will is actually for

Ontario levies estate administration tax on the value of the estate probated. Some assets — notably private company shares, and personal property whose transfer does not require a third party to see a probated will — can often be transferred without probate at all. A multiple wills structure separates those assets into their own will, so their value never enters the probate calculation.

How the two wills divide the estate

The primary will covers assets that will need probate: real estate held in the deceased's name alone, bank and investment accounts, and anything where an institution will insist on seeing a court-authenticated document before releasing it. The secondary will covers the assets that do not need it, most commonly shares in a private corporation, shareholder loans, and personal effects.

Where it goes wrong

Multiple wills have to be drafted as a deliberate pair. A later will containing a general revocation clause can accidentally revoke the earlier one, and the two documents need to appoint their estates trustees and allocate assets in a way that does not leave gaps or overlaps. Estates litigation in this area usually traces back to drafting that treated the second will as an afterthought.

Who this is worth considering for

The structure is most useful where there is a private corporation in the picture, which is why it comes up so often for business owners and incorporated professionals. For an estate made up mostly of a house and bank accounts, there may be nothing meaningful to keep out of probate.

This article is general information about Ontario estate planning, not legal advice, and it is not a summary of the firm's own writing. Estate administration tax rules and the treatment of particular assets change — speak to a lawyer about your own situation.
