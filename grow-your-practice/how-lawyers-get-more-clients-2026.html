How Lawyers Get More Clients in Canada: A 2026 Guide

Practice Growth

Most lawyers didn't go to law school to become a marketer, and most firms don't have a marketing department deciding where the next client comes from. That job usually falls to a partner, an office manager, or the lawyer themselves, squeezed in between actual files. This is a working guide to where new clients in Canada actually come from in 2026, roughly in the order a small or mid-size firm should tackle them.

Start with where people already look

Before spending on anything, it's worth being honest about where a prospective client's search actually starts. For most practice areas it's a Google search, either for a lawyer by name (someone referred them) or by need - "family lawyer Ottawa" or "immigration lawyer Montreal." The firms that show up consistently across a Google Business Profile, a handful of relevant directories, and their own website tend to win a disproportionate share of that traffic, simply because they appear more than once on the same results page.

Google Business Profile: the foundation, not an afterthought

A complete, accurate Google Business Profile is free and it's usually the single highest-leverage thing an individual firm can control. Right category, correct service area, real photos, and hours that are actually kept up to date all feed into whether you show up in the local map pack for searches in your city. We cover the specifics in our Google Business Profile guide, but the short version is: claim it, verify it, and treat it like a page on your website rather than a one-time setup task.

Directories still compound

Legal directories get dismissed by some firms as outdated, but they still do two things well: they give you a second (and third, and fourth) listing in the results for searches you're not ranking for organically yet, and they build the kind of consistent citation data (name, address, phone number) that supports your local SEO. The trick is being selective rather than listing on everything - see our breakdown of which legal directories are actually worth it in Canada.

Referrals: still the highest-converting source for most firms

Ask most established firms where their best clients come from and the honest answer is still referrals - from past clients, from other lawyers in adjacent practice areas, from accountants and real estate agents and financial advisors who run into legal problems in their own client base. Referral relationships take longer to build than a directory listing or an ad campaign, but they close at a higher rate and cost nothing per lead. We go deeper on how to build this deliberately in building a referral network as a lawyer.

Content and SEO: slower, but it compounds

A website with real, specific content about the kinds of cases you handle - not just a one-paragraph description of "family law services" - gives Google more to work with and gives prospective clients more reason to trust you before they call. This is a longer game than paid ads, typically months rather than weeks, but the traffic it earns doesn't stop the day you stop paying for it. Our SEO for lawyers guide covers where to start.

Paid ads: useful, but know the economics first

Google Ads can put a firm in front of people searching right now, which matters most for urgency-driven practice areas like criminal defence or personal injury. It's also expensive - legal keywords are consistently among the highest cost-per-click categories on the platform - so it works best once you know what a client is actually worth to your firm and can afford to lose money on some clicks while you learn what converts. See should your law firm advertise on Google for realistic budget numbers.

Know your province's advertising rules before you do any of this

Every provincial law society and the Barreau du Québec and Chambre des notaires du Québec have rules about how lawyers can advertise - what you can claim, how you can use client testimonials, and in Quebec, language requirements under the Charter of the French Language that don't apply anywhere else in Canada. It's worth a quick read of your own regulator's marketing and advertising guidelines before you launch anything at scale, and we cover the practical differences by province in how lawyer marketing differs across Ontario, Quebec, and BC.

Where to actually start this week

If you're doing none of this yet: claim and complete your Google Business Profile first, it takes an afternoon and it's free. Then get listed on one or two directories relevant to your province and practice area. Then start building a referral habit - one coffee or call a week with someone whose clients overlap with yours. Ads and content can come once those three are solid.
