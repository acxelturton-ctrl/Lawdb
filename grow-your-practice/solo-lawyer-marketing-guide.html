Solo Lawyer Marketing: How to Compete With Bigger Firms

Solo Practice

A solo lawyer isn't going to outspend a firm with a marketing budget and a dedicated intake team, and trying to compete on every channel at once usually means doing all of them poorly. The firms that do well as solo practices tend to pick a small number of channels and actually execute on them consistently, rather than spreading thin.

The advantage a solo lawyer actually has

A solo practice can respond to an inquiry personally, same day, in a way a larger firm's intake process often can't match. That speed and personal attention is a genuine competitive advantage worth building marketing around - a promise of "you'll talk to the lawyer, not an intake coordinator" is a real differentiator, not just marketing copy, when it's true.

Pick two channels, not six

A realistic starting point for a solo lawyer with limited time is one free channel that compounds (a complete Google Business Profile, since it costs nothing but time) and one that produces inquiries faster (a directory listing or a small, tightly targeted ad budget). Trying to also run a content calendar, a social media presence, and a referral program on top of an active caseload usually means none of them get done well.

Being the obvious choice for one specific thing

A generalist solo practice competing against ten other generalist solo practices in the same city has a harder time standing out than one that's clearly positioned around a specific niche - wills for young families, employment law for tech workers, immigration for a specific community. Niching down narrows the pool of competitors dramatically, even if it also narrows the pool of potential clients; the tradeoff is usually worth it for a one-person shop.

Content that's realistic on a solo schedule

A blog post a month, or even one every two months, written from real experience with real (anonymized) situations, will do more for a solo practice's credibility over a year than an ambitious content calendar abandoned after six weeks because there was no time left after actual case work. Consistency at a sustainable pace beats an unsustainable pace that stops.

Where directories fit for a one-person shop

A directory listing does something a solo lawyer often can't do alone: put the firm in front of people who are actively comparing a few options, without requiring ongoing time investment once it's set up. A free listing costs nothing but ten minutes; listing your firm on LawDB is a reasonable first step alongside a completed Google Business Profile.
