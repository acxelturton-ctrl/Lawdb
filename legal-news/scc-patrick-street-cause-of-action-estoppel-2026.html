Can You Sue Over Something You Could Have Raised Earlier? Supreme Court Clarifies Cause of Action Estoppel

Civil Litigation

On May 8, 2026, the Supreme Court of Canada released its decision in Patrick Street Holdings Ltd. v. 11368 NL Inc., clarifying when a court will refuse to hear a second claim because it should have been brought in an earlier proceeding.

The background

In 2016, 11368 NL Inc. defaulted on a mortgage held by Patrick Street, which sold the property under power of sale and bought it. Patrick Street divided the proceeds among several mortgages, including a $4 million collateral mortgage. Two creditors challenged the accounting, and the judge excluded the collateral mortgage. In 2019, 11368 applied for the remaining proceeds, and the courts below held the claim was barred by res judicata.

What the Court decided

The Court dismissed the appeal. Cause of action estoppel applies where: (1) there was a final decision of a court of competent jurisdiction; (2) the parties are the same; (3) the later cause of action is not separate and distinct; and (4) the basis of the new claim was, or could have been, argued with reasonable diligence in the earlier proceeding.

There is a narrow residual discretion to refuse to apply the doctrine if it would cause injustice. The doctrine must be raised at the earliest opportunity, but need not be named if the material facts are pleaded. The majority reasons were written by Chief Justice Wagner; Justice Martin (with Justice Karakatsanis concurring) and Justice Côté dissented on different grounds.

Why this matters

For anyone involved in litigation, the lesson is to raise every related claim in the first proceeding. Holding a claim back for later can mean losing it entirely.
