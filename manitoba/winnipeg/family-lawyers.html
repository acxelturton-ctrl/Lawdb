What family law lawyers in Winnipeg help with

Family lawyers advise on separation and divorce, parenting arrangements, child and spousal support, and the division of property. Many also prepare separation agreements and marriage contracts.

  • Separation and divorce
  • Parenting time and decision-making for children
  • Child and spousal support, including changes to support
  • Dividing property and debt
  • Marriage contracts and separation agreements
  • Mediation, collaborative law and court proceedings

Family law in Manitoba: what to know

Manitoba's Limitation of Actions Act sets a basic two-year limitation period for most claims, with exceptions. Speak to a lawyer early so that a deadline does not run out.

Divorce is governed by the federal Divorce Act, while property division and some other matters are decided under provincial law, so both matter in most separations.

How to choose a family law lawyer in Winnipeg

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Law Society of Manitoba.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is family law, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • Do you recommend mediation or a negotiated agreement before court?
  • How does your retainer work, and what could this cost overall?
  • What should I and should I not do before we meet again?
  • How do you approach disputes about the children?
  • Can you also help with the financial disclosure?
  • How long does a case like mine usually take?

What to bring

  • Dates of marriage, cohabitation and separation
  • Details of the children and current parenting arrangements
  • Recent tax returns, pay stubs and account statements
  • A list of property and debts
  • Any existing agreements or court orders

How the process usually works

  1. You explain your situation and your goals.
  2. The lawyer advises on rights and options and sets out a plan.
  3. Financial and other information is exchanged.
  4. Settlement is explored through negotiation or mediation.
  5. If needed, the matter proceeds to court for a decision.

Costs and fees

Family lawyers usually require an upfront retainer and bill hourly. Ask how fees change if the case settles early, and whether limited-scope help is offered. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
