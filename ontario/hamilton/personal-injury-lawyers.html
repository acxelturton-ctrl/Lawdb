What personal injury lawyers in Hamilton help with

Personal injury lawyers act for people hurt in car accidents, falls and other incidents, and in disability insurance disputes. Many work on contingency, meaning fees are paid from the recovery, which is worth confirming in writing.

  • Injuries from a car, bike or pedestrian accident
  • Slip, trip and fall injuries on someone else's property
  • Long-term disability insurance claims that were denied
  • Dealing with an insurer about benefits
  • Medical malpractice or other professional negligence
  • Claims involving a serious or catastrophic injury

Personal Injury law in Ontario: what to know

Ontario's Limitations Act, 2002 sets a basic two-year limitation period for most civil claims, with important exceptions. Speak to a lawyer early so that a deadline does not run out.

Car-accident claims in Ontario usually involve statutory accident benefits from your own insurer in addition to any claim against another driver, and each has its own deadlines.

How to choose a personal injury lawyer in Hamilton

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Law Society of Ontario.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is personal injury, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • Do you work on contingency, and what percentage and expenses apply?
  • What happens to fees and expenses if the claim is not successful?
  • Which deadlines apply to my claim?
  • Do you also act for insurers, or only for injured people?
  • Who will handle my file day to day?
  • How do you estimate the value of a claim like mine?

What to bring

  • The accident or incident report and any police report
  • Photos of the scene and your injuries
  • Medical records and a list of treatment
  • Any letters or forms from your insurer
  • Proof of lost income and expenses

How the process usually works

  1. You tell the lawyer what happened and share your records.
  2. The lawyer helps you access benefits and gathers medical evidence.
  3. A claim is prepared once your recovery is clearer.
  4. Negotiation or mediation with the insurer follows.
  5. If there is no settlement, the case can go to trial.

Costs and fees

Many injury lawyers are paid from the recovery as a percentage, called a contingency fee. Always ask what is deducted for expenses as well. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
