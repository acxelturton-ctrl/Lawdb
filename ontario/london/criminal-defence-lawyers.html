What criminal defence lawyers in London help with

Criminal defence lawyers represent people charged with offences, from impaired driving and assault to fraud and drug charges, at every stage from the first police contact to trial or appeal.

  • Being charged or under investigation
  • Bail and release conditions
  • Impaired driving and other driving offences
  • Assault, theft, fraud and drug charges
  • Trials, plea negotiations and sentencing
  • Appeals and record-related applications

Criminal Defence law in Ontario: what to know

If cost is a concern, you may qualify for help from Legal Aid Ontario. A defence lawyer can also advise you whether it makes sense to apply.

How to choose a criminal defence lawyer in London

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Law Society of Ontario.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is criminal defence, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • Have you handled charges like mine before?
  • What are the likely outcomes and the main risks?
  • How and when will we talk about disclosure from the Crown?
  • What are your fees, and what is covered?
  • What should I avoid saying or doing in the meantime?
  • Do you also appear in the court where I am charged?

What to bring

  • Any charging documents, release papers or court dates
  • Details of the incident and anything the police gave you
  • The names of witnesses
  • Any messages or records relevant to the charge
  • Your ID and contact details

How the process usually works

  1. The lawyer gets the charges and the disclosure from the Crown.
  2. They advise you on bail, evidence and options.
  3. Pre-trial discussions with the Crown may lead to a resolution.
  4. If there is no resolution, the case goes to trial.
  5. After a verdict there may be sentencing or an appeal.

Costs and fees

Criminal defence is often quoted as a flat fee per stage, or a retainer billed hourly. If cost is a concern, ask whether you may qualify for legal aid. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
