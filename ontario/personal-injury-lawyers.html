About personal injury law in Ontario

Car accidents, slip and falls, and long-term disability denials are the most common reasons people in Ontario end up looking for a personal injury lawyer. Nearly all of these firms work on contingency, meaning you don't pay legal fees unless they recover money for you - which is worth confirming directly with any firm before signing on.

Insurance companies have adjusters and lawyers working the file from day one; having your own representation, especially before you give a recorded statement or sign anything, tends to matter more than people expect. Ontario's accident benefits system is also more complicated than most provinces', with strict deadlines for certain claims, so timing matters. Personal Injury lawyers in Ontario: a short guide

What personal injury lawyers help with

  • Car, truck, motorcycle and bicycle collisions
  • Slip and fall and other injuries on someone else's property
  • Long-term disability and insurance claims
  • Dealing with insurers after an accident
  • Serious or catastrophic injuries
  • Fatal accident claims for families

How to choose a personal injury lawyer

  1. Look for a firm that focuses on injury claims and has taken cases like yours to a settlement or trial.
  2. Ask how the firm is paid and what happens if the claim does not succeed.
  3. Read recent reviews for communication, since claims can take a long time.
  4. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society.

Questions to ask at your first call

  • How do you charge, and what costs could I owe along the way?
  • What time limits apply to my claim?
  • What evidence should I be keeping now?
  • How long do claims like mine usually take?
  • Who will work on my file, and how will I get updates?

What to bring

  • Any accident or police report
  • Photos of the scene, vehicles and injuries
  • Medical records, or the names of the doctors and clinics you have seen
  • Insurance details and letters from insurers
  • Records of time off work and any other costs
