About traffic ticket law in Ontario

A speeding ticket you can usually handle yourself, but the more serious end of Ontario's Highway Traffic Act - careless driving, stunt driving, driving while suspended, or a criminal-adjacent charge like impaired driving - carries real consequences: demerit points, licence suspension, insurance increases that last years, and in some cases a criminal record. These charges are prosecuted the same way a criminal charge would be, which is why they're almost always handled by the same lawyers who do criminal defence work rather than a separate "traffic lawyer" specialty.

A lawyer familiar with the local courthouse and prosecutor's office can often negotiate a reduced charge, and in some cases get a matter withdrawn entirely - something that's much harder to do without legal representation. The firms below have handled traffic and provincial offence matters in Ontario; if you know which courthouse your matter is being heard at, use the location tool below to find someone local to it.

Traffic Tickets lawyers in Ontario: a short guide

What traffic ticket lawyers help with

  • Speeding and other traffic tickets
  • Stunt driving and excessive speed charges
  • Careless and dangerous driving charges
  • Driving while suspended
  • Demerit points and licence suspensions
  • Impaired driving charges, usually alongside criminal defence counsel

How to choose a traffic ticket lawyer

  1. Find out whether your charge is a ticket or a more serious offence, since that affects who you need.
  2. Ask whether the firm appears at the courthouse where your matter will be heard.
  3. Check the firm's rating and recent reviews, and how quickly it responds, because court dates and deadlines can be short.
  4. Confirm that whoever represents you is licensed with the provincial law society or another authorised regulator.

Questions to ask at your first call

  • What are the possible outcomes for my charge?
  • What would a conviction mean for my licence, insurance and record?
  • Do I need to attend court myself?
  • What is your fee, and what is included?
  • How soon do I need to act?

What to bring

  • The ticket or summons and any court notices
  • Your driver's licence
  • Your driving record, if you have it
  • Photos, dashcam video or notes about what happened
  • Insurance details, if the charge involves a collision
