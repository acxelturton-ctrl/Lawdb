About wills and estates law in Ontario

A will drafted with a lawyer is harder to challenge and less likely to leave your family sorting out ambiguity later, compared to a template downloaded online - particularly if you own a home, have kids from more than one relationship, or run a business. Most wills lawyers in Ontario also handle powers of attorney for property and personal care at the same time, since the three documents are usually done together.

On the other side of it, if you're named an executor or are dealing with probate for someone who has passed, an estates lawyer can walk you through what's actually required - probate isn't always necessary, and a lawyer can tell you early whether it applies to your situation. The firms listed here handle wills, estates, and powers of attorney across Ontario.

Wills & Estates lawyers in Ontario: a short guide

What wills and estates lawyers help with

  • Wills and powers of attorney
  • Estate planning, including trusts
  • Applying to administer an estate after a death
  • Estate disputes and contested wills
  • Guardianship and capacity questions
  • Updating documents after a marriage, separation or birth

How to choose a wills and estates lawyer

  1. Decide whether you need documents drawn up, or help with an estate after someone has died, since firms often lean one way.
  2. Ask how the firm handles a family with property in more than one province or country.
  3. Read recent client reviews for patience and clear explanations, because these matters are personal.
  4. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society.

Questions to ask at your first call

  • Which documents do I need, and why?
  • What happens to my estate if I do not have a will?
  • How should I choose who acts for me, and what does that role involve?
  • How often should these documents be reviewed?
  • Where should the originals be kept?

What to bring

  • A list of your assets, debts and insurance
  • The names and contact details of the people you want to name
  • Any existing wills or powers of attorney
  • Photo identification
  • For an estate: the death certificate and the will, if there is one
