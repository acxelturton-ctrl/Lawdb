Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 20, 2026

LawDB.ca ("LawDB," "we," "us") operates a directory of Canadian law firms. This policy explains what personal information we collect through this website, how we use it, who we share it with, and how long we keep it. It applies to visitors browsing the directory, people who send a consultation request or report a listing, and law firms and their staff who create an account, manage a listing or subscribe to a Featured plan.

Information we collect

  • Consultation requests: when you fill out the "Request a Consultation" form on a firm's page, we collect your name, email address, phone number (if provided), the message you write, and the page you sent it from.
  • Reports about a listing: if you report a listing, we collect your name, email address and what you tell us.
  • Firm accounts: if you create an account to manage a firm, we collect your email address; the name, job title and note you give when you ask to manage a firm; the information you enter about the firm (which becomes public once approved); and a record of sign-ins and of what you change and when. If you upload pictures (a logo, cover or photos), we store them and show them publicly on the firm's listing and page.
  • Sign-in with Google or LinkedIn: if you choose it, the provider tells us your name, your verified email address and the address of your profile picture (nothing else: not your password, contacts, connections, job history or activity) so we can create or find your account. We then ask whether you want to use that picture as your LawDB profile photo. We copy it only if you say yes; until you answer we keep only its address, and if you say no we do not ask again.
  • Profile photo and cover photo: if you add them, we store the pictures (shrunk to a small square and a wide banner) so they can show on your own account pages. It is private to you; it is not shown to other members or to firms. You can remove it at any time.
  • Your profile: in the welcome steps after you sign up (all can be skipped or changed later) you can tell us your name, your city and province, your most recent job title and employer, and what brings you to LawDB. We use it to personalise your dashboard and emails. It is private: it is not shown to other members or to firms.
  • Ratings: if you rate a firm, we store your star rating and any comment, linked to your account. A comment that we approve is shown publicly without your name or email.
  • Following and saving: if you sign in to follow law firms or save stories, we store which firms and stories you chose, so we can show you your feed, and when you last opened your feed (to show a dot when there is something new). Only you can see this list. A firm sees only the total number of people who follow it, never who they are.
  • Your location (only if you ask): if you tap “Lawyers near me” or the location button on a map, your browser asks your permission, and the position it gives is used in your browser to work out which firms are closest. It is not sent to us or stored on our servers. The home page keeps it in that browser tab for up to 30 minutes so the results page can use it; it is gone when you close the tab. If you type a town instead, the town's name may appear in the address of the results page.
  • Visit and search counts: to see which pages are useful, and which towns and practice areas to add next, we count visits ourselves, without tracking anyone. When a page opens, when you tap a button such as Call, Website or Directions, or when you search, your browser sends us a short message that we add to a daily running total: the page, whether you are on a phone or a computer, which website sent you, your country and province as worked out by Cloudflare (never your street or address), and for a search the kind of lawyer and the place. If a search names a town we have no page for, or matches nothing, we also keep the words typed (only when they are plain letters, never numbers, email addresses or web links). We do not store your network address, your browser details, a cookie or any identifier with these counts, so they cannot be traced back to you, and robots are not counted. To tell a new visit from another page of the same visit, your browser keeps a small note in that browser tab only (not a cookie); it disappears when you close the tab. Nothing is sent if your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal.
  • Abuse-prevention data: when you submit a form or request a sign-in link, we keep a one-way scrambled (hashed) form of your network address, only to limit spam and abuse. It is deleted automatically after 7 days.
  • Blog excerpts: for firms whose websites publish a public blog feed, we read the headline, date, a short excerpt and the link of the latest posts and show them on the firm's page and on LawDB News, linking to the full article on the firm's own website. This is business content, not personal information about visitors.
  • Directory listing data: the law firm information shown on this site (name, address, phone number, practice areas, rating, review count) comes from Google's public business listings or from the firms themselves, not from individual consumers.
  • Payment information (law firms only): if a firm subscribes to a Featured plan, payment is processed entirely by Stripe. We do not receive or store your card details.
  • Scheduling information (law firms only): if you book a call with us through Calendly, Calendly collects the scheduling details you provide.

We do not use advertising cookies, third-party analytics or any technology that follows you around the web; the only counting is our own, described above. The only cookie we set is a sign-in cookie for firm owners and administrators who sign in; it is removed when you sign out, or after 14 days.

How we use your information

  • Consultation requests: we share the request with the specific law firm your inquiry concerns, by email and in that firm's LawDB dashboard, so it can respond to you directly. We also count, in aggregate, how many inquiries a firm receives (for example, "you received 6 inquiries this month"); we do not share the content of other people's messages as part of that.
  • Reports: we use them only to review the listing concerned and, if needed, to contact you. We do not share them with the firm.
  • Following and saving: to show you the stories of the firms you follow and the stories you saved, and to show each firm how many people follow it.
  • Firm accounts: to check that you are authorised to manage a firm, let you manage it, and email you about your requests and about enquiries to your firm.
  • Safety and quality: to prevent abuse, investigate problems, and keep a record of changes made to listings.

Who we share it with

We share a consultation request with the law firm it concerns (and with that firm's approved account holders). We also use the following services, which process data on our behalf under their own privacy terms:

  • Cloudflare: hosts this website and stores our database and backups;
  • Brevo: sends our emails (sign-in links, approval notices, and enquiry notifications to firms);
  • Web3Forms: some firm pages also send consultation requests through Web3Forms, which delivers them to us by email;
  • OpenStreetMap (Nominatim): when a new firm is added, its business address is sent to find its position on the map;
  • Stripe: processes payments for law firms subscribing to a Featured plan;
  • Calendly: handles scheduling for calls booked with us.

We do not sell your personal information. We may disclose information if the law requires it.

Where your information is stored

Our service providers may store or process information outside your province or outside Canada, including in the United States. If you live in Quebec, this means your personal information may be transferred outside Quebec. By using the site you understand this.

How long we keep it

  • Consultation requests and reports: your name, email, phone number and message are kept for 12 months and then removed automatically. We keep only which firm received an inquiry, when, and its status, so inquiry counts still add up.
  • Firm accounts: kept while the account is in use, and removed on request.
  • Ratings and comments: kept while the account is in use, and removed on request (you can also remove your own rating yourself at any time).
  • Firms you follow and stories you saved: kept while the account is in use, removed on request, and removed with the account.
  • Pictures: kept until you or we remove them; once removed they stop being shown, and the file is deleted from storage.
  • Activity records: kept for 24 months.
  • Visit and search counts: the daily totals are kept for 400 days; the towns and unmatched search words that go with a search are kept for 90 days. Both are deleted automatically.
  • Sign-in links: expire after 15 minutes and are deleted within a day. Sign-in cookies last up to 14 days.
  • Backups: nightly copies are kept for 35 days and then expire automatically, so a deletion shows up in backups within that time.
  • Web3Forms copies: kept for up to 30 days under that provider's plan.

Your rights

You can ask us what personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete it, or withdraw your consent, by contacting us at the address below. We will respond within 30 days. If you are in Quebec and are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec; elsewhere in Canada, you may contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or your provincial regulator.

Children's privacy

This site is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under the age of majority in their province or territory.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. The "last updated" date at the top of this page will reflect the most recent revision.

Contact us

LawDB's operator is responsible for the protection of personal information on this site. Questions about this policy, or requests regarding your personal information, can be sent to privacy@lawdb.ca.

This policy describes our current practices in plain language. It isn't a substitute for professional legal advice, and if your business has specific compliance obligations (for example, under PIPEDA or a provincial equivalent), it's worth having a lawyer review this before you rely on it.
