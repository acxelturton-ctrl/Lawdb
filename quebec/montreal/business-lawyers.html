What business lawyers in Montreal help with

Business lawyers help with incorporation, shareholder and partnership agreements, commercial contracts, financing, and buying or selling a business. Larger firms also act on mergers and acquisitions.

  • Incorporating a company and setting up share structure
  • Drafting shareholder or partnership agreements
  • Reviewing commercial contracts and leases
  • Buying or selling a business or its assets
  • Raising money from investors or lenders
  • Resolving a dispute with a partner, supplier or customer

Business law in Quebec: what to know

A company can be incorporated provincially or federally, and which route is better depends on where you operate and your plans.

Quebec businesses should also check the province's French-language requirements, which affect names, signage and some documents.

How to choose a business lawyer in Montreal

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Barreau du Québec.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is business, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • Do you regularly work with businesses of my size and industry?
  • Is the work quoted as a flat fee or hourly?
  • Should I incorporate provincially or federally?
  • How do you protect my interests in a shareholder agreement?
  • Can you coordinate with my accountant?
  • What would you flag as the biggest risk in this deal?

What to bring

  • A short description of the business or deal
  • Existing contracts, leases or corporate records
  • Names and ownership split of the owners
  • Your accountant's contact details
  • Any draft agreement the other side has sent

How the process usually works

  1. You explain the goal and the key people involved.
  2. The lawyer proposes a structure and gives you a fee estimate.
  3. Documents are drafted, negotiated and revised.
  4. You sign and the lawyer files any required registrations.
  5. The firm stores the corporate records or hands them to you.

Costs and fees

Incorporation is commonly a flat fee. Deals and negotiations are usually hourly or capped, so ask for an estimate with the main assumptions. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.

How long do I have to act on a business matter in Quebec?

Quebec's Civil Code sets a general three-year prescription period for most personal claims, with exceptions. Because these deadlines can be short, it is safest to speak to a lawyer as soon as possible.
