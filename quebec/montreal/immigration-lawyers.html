What immigration lawyers in Montreal help with

Immigration lawyers help with work and study permits, permanent residence, family sponsorship, citizenship and appeals of refused applications.

  • Work and study permits
  • Permanent residence applications
  • Family sponsorship
  • Refugee and humanitarian claims
  • Citizenship applications
  • Appeals or reviews of refusals

Immigration law in Quebec: what to know

Immigration applications are decided by the federal government, and you can only be represented for a fee by a lawyer, a licensed immigration consultant or, in Quebec, a notary.

Quebec chooses many of its own economic immigrants, so some applications involve a provincial step before the federal one.

How to choose a immigration lawyer in Montreal

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Barreau du Québec.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is immigration, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • Who exactly will be representing me, and are they licensed?
  • What are the realistic chances and the main risks?
  • What are the government fees and your own fees?
  • How long does a case like mine take?
  • What documents do I need to start?
  • What happens if my application is refused?

What to bring

  • Your passport and travel documents
  • Any previous applications and decisions
  • Education and work records
  • Proof of status in Canada
  • Documents about family members involved

How the process usually works

  1. You share your situation and goals.
  2. The lawyer assesses the best route and sets out a timeline.
  3. Documents are collected and the application is prepared.
  4. The application is submitted and you receive updates.
  5. If there is a refusal, they advise on next steps.

Costs and fees

Immigration work is typically quoted as a flat fee per application. Confirm what is included and who pays government fees. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
