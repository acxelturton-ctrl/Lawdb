About real estate law in Quebec

This page is about property matters that have gone wrong. If you are simply buying or selling a home in Quebec, you need a notaire, not a lawyer. The transfer of property here is done by notarial deed, and the notaire verifies title, prepares the deed of sale, handles the mortgage paperwork, and manages the funds at closing. That is not work an avocat does.

A lawyer becomes the right call once there is a dispute. The most common is a hidden defect claim (vice cache), where a buyer discovers a serious problem the seller did not disclose, which Quebec law treats seriously and with strict notice deadlines. Others include a broken promise to purchase, disagreements with a condo syndicate or co-owners, construction and renovation disputes, and boundary or servitude problems with a neighbour. These are civil litigation matters under the Code civil du Quebec, and the firms below handle them across the province.

Real Estate lawyers in Quebec: a short guide

What real estate lawyers help with

  • Buying or selling a home, condo or cottage
  • Reviewing an agreement of purchase and sale before you sign
  • Title searches, title insurance and closing
  • Mortgages, refinancing and private lending
  • Landlord and tenant matters
  • Commercial property purchases and leases

How to choose a real estate lawyer

  1. Look for a firm that handles the kind of property you are dealing with, such as a condo, a rural property or a commercial building.
  2. Ask who will actually do the work and how closings are scheduled around your possession date.
  3. Check the firm's Google rating and read what recent clients say about communication.
  4. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society before you send documents or money.

Questions to ask at your first call

  • Who will handle my file, and who can I call if they are away?
  • What is included in your fee, and which costs are paid on top of it?
  • How long will the closing take, and what could delay it?
  • Do you need anything from me before the closing date?
  • Have you handled a property like this one before?

What to bring

  • The agreement of purchase and sale, or the listing and offer if you have not signed
  • Photo identification
  • Mortgage approval or lender details
  • Condo documents, if the property is a condo
  • Any survey, title or inspection reports you already have
⚖

Check before you hire. You can confirm that a lawyer is licensed with the Barreau du Québec. If cost is a worry, ask whether the Commission des services juridiques (legal aid) could help with your matter. LawDB is an independent directory: this page is general information, not legal advice.

Common questions

How do I choose a real estate lawyer in Quebec?

Look for a firm that handles the kind of property you are dealing with, such as a condo, a rural property or a commercial building. Confirm the lawyer is licensed with the provincial law society before you send documents or money.

What does a real estate lawyer cost in Quebec?

Real estate lawyers usually quote a fee for the work plus separate costs paid to others, such as title insurance, registration and taxes. Ask each firm for a written quote that lists both.
