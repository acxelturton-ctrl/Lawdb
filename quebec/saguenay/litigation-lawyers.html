What litigation lawyers in Saguenay help with

Litigation lawyers act in civil disputes: contract and commercial claims, shareholder and partnership disputes, debt recovery and other cases that may end up in court or settled before trial.

  • Suing or defending a contract or debt claim
  • Shareholder, partnership or business disputes
  • Construction and property disputes
  • Applying for or opposing an injunction
  • Appealing a court decision
  • Negotiating a settlement or going to mediation

Litigation law in Quebec: what to know

Quebec's Civil Code sets a general three-year prescription period for most personal claims, with exceptions. Speak to a lawyer early so that a deadline does not run out.

Civil disputes in Quebec are heard in the Court of Québec and the Superior Court of Québec.

How to choose a litigation lawyer in Saguenay

  1. Confirm the lawyer is licensed and in good standing with the Barreau du Québec.
  2. Ask how much of their practice is litigation, and how recent their experience is with cases like yours.
  3. Compare the Google rating together with the number of reviews, not the rating alone.
  4. Read recent reviews for comments on communication and responsiveness.
  5. Ask for the fee structure in writing before you sign, including what is billed separately.
  6. Meet with more than one firm if the matter is large, and trust the one that explains your options clearly.

Questions to ask at your first consultation

  • What are the realistic outcomes and costs, not only the best case?
  • Can we take it in stages so I can reassess as the case develops?
  • Do you recommend mediation or settlement first?
  • Who will do the day-to-day work on my file?
  • How are disbursements and expert fees handled?
  • What are the main deadlines in my case?

What to bring

  • All contracts, emails and messages about the dispute
  • A timeline of key events
  • Any demand letters, claims or court documents you have received
  • Names of witnesses
  • Records of money paid or owed

How the process usually works

  1. The lawyer reviews your documents and explains your options.
  2. A demand letter or statement of claim (or defence) is prepared.
  3. Both sides exchange documents and information.
  4. Mediation or settlement talks often take place.
  5. If the case does not settle, it proceeds to trial and a decision.

Costs and fees

Litigation is most often billed hourly. Ask for a budget by stage, and what happens to fees if the case settles early. Ask about disbursements (third-party costs), taxes, and what happens if your matter changes in scope. Many firms offer a first consultation, but confirm whether it is free.
